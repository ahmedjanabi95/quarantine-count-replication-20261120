# Prospective adaptive read comparison
All arms use the same closed-epoch benign diversion experiment, kernel and userspace datapaths, bursts of 4 or 32, and two receiving-port meters. Each site shuffles complete matched settings before execution. Frozen timers: workstation 325 ms (unqualified shorter candidate) and 400 ms (fallback); hosted 300 ms (previously qualified at that site, new runner). The original 20-ms checker is unchanged. Both new policies first schedule a read 100 ms after the seal, require a complete operational witness, and reserve a last opportunity at 460 ms with a 500-ms read-start deadline. The sparse control waits 100 ms after mismatches. The adaptive policy waits 35 + 110 times the clipped missing fraction in milliseconds after each mismatch (145 ms if no reading). Never infer a correct count from the schedule. Final release and diagnostics stay unchanged. No training or updating across trials.
Six new clean schedule seeds (1811–1816) each cover four settings per policy. Two different omission seeds (1817–1818) cover eight fault trials per policy. Report all planned, eligible, correct, wrong and withheld outcomes. For clean matched settings report total reads per policy, percent reduction from current checking, paired guard/checker/sparse versus adaptive retirement times and reads (comparator minus adaptive). Average four settings per complete seed, then enumerate six-block percentile bootstrap intervals. Separate omissions and clean observations. Primary practical criterion: fewer clean reads with at least as many correct clean releases and no additional wrong reports as current checking at each site. No claim of population noninferiority. Distinguish effects shared with sparse scheduling from benefits attributable to the deficit rule. Record maintenance/update durations and retain late or missing observations and failed attempts.
